# PD3 Speaking Format: What Actually Happens

## TL;DR

- The June 2026 oral period is 10-23 June 2026.
- The oral exam has two parts of about five minutes each.
- Part 1 is prepared: a two-minute presentation plus follow-up questions.
- Part 2 is unseen: draw one topic, look at pictures briefly, then answer concrete and broad questions.
- You are judged on clear B2 communication, not specialist knowledge.

## What is it?

PD3 speaking is a short test of whether you can explain, argue, and react in Danish about ordinary Danish society topics.

Part 1 is like a mini presentation. You get a topic before the exam, prepare a two-minute talk, then answer questions.

Part 2 is like a controlled conversation. You draw a topic, see two pictures, and answer questions that move from concrete details to a wider society angle.

## Why it matters

The test is short, so structure matters. A fluent but messy answer can sound weaker than a simpler answer with a clear beginning, two arguments, one contrast, and a conclusion.

## Source-backed format

Official Dansk og Prøver says:

- Part 1 topic is prepared before the exam.
- Part 2 topic is unknown and drawn from three possible topics.
- Both topics are societal issues.
- The examiner can ask you to clarify, explain, expand, justify, and take a position.

Clavis adds a practical timing summary:

- 15 minutes including grading.
- Part 1: 2 minutes presentation plus 3 minutes follow-up.
- Part 2: about 10 seconds for pictures, then about 4.5 minutes of questions.

Studieskolen and UCplus confirm the June 2026 oral window as 10-23 June 2026.

## Common pitfalls

- Memorizing a full text and sounding unnatural.
- Talking only about the picture instead of the topic.
- Giving yes/no answers.
- Forgetting to answer "why".
- Staying personal when the examiner moves to a society-level question.

## Hands-on exercises

1. Time a two-minute presentation about workplace stress.
2. Cut it to three points: problem, cause, consequence.
3. Add one "discussion hook", for example: "Det er dog ikke så enkelt..."
4. Practice asking for clarification: "Mener du, om...?"
