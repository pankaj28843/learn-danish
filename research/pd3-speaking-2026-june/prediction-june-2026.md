# June 2026 PD3 Speaking Topic Prediction

Updated: 2026-05-25T13:14:58Z

## Prediction Method

This forecast weights:

1. Official examples from Dansk og Prøver.
2. Public learner reports from PD3 oral exams.
3. Language-school and teacher course-prep pages.
4. Recurrent PD3 practice topics from recent websites.
5. Breadth and reusability as B2 societal-discussion topics.

It does not claim access to unreleased SIRI material.

## Highest Probability

### 1. Work, Job Search, and Workplace Stress

Expected forms:

- Jobsøgning og ledighed.
- Stress på arbejdspladsen.
- Fleksible arbejdstider.
- At skifte job.
- Arbejdsmiljø and livskvalitet.

Why: Official examples include job search/unemployment. LWiD mentions stress/workplace and changing jobs. Learn Danish Lab lists workplace stress, work environment, flexible hours, and unwritten workplace rules.

Practice stance:

- Discuss individual responsibility vs employer/state responsibility.
- Mention work-life balance, sick leave, productivity, and mental health.

### 2. Social Media, Digital Life, and Children Online

Expected forms:

- Brug af sociale medier.
- Nyheder på sociale medier.
- Privatliv på nettet.
- Børn og unge online.
- Digital undervisning or technology in everyday life.

Why: Official Dansk og Prøver uses social media as a Part 2 example. Learn Danish Lab lists children online, technology, and digital education. LWiD lists social media and private life.

Practice stance:

- Benefits: community, information, flexibility.
- Risks: misinformation, privacy, pressure, addiction.

### 3. Health, Welfare, and Lifestyle

Expected forms:

- Livsstilssygdomme, diabetes.
- Sundhedstilbud på arbejdspladsen.
- Brugerbetaling i sundhedssystemet.
- Ulighed i sundhed.
- Stress as health problem.

Why: Official Part 1 example includes lifestyle diseases and diabetes. Official written example mentions workplace health offers. Learn Danish Lab and AB Danish both emphasize healthcare/welfare themes.

Practice stance:

- Public responsibility vs personal responsibility.
- Prevention vs treatment.
- Equality in access to care.

### 4. School, Children, and Youth

Expected forms:

- Den danske folkeskole and mobning.
- Digital education.
- Youth finance.
- Young people and alcohol.
- Childcare institutions.

Why: Official Part 1 example includes school and bullying. Life in Aalborg reports young Danes' drinking habits. Learn Danish PDF uses childcare institutions as the model presentation.

Practice stance:

- Parent/school/state responsibility.
- Freedom vs protection.
- Digital tools vs social development.

### 5. Climate, Environment, and Transport

Expected forms:

- Klimabekymringer and bæredygtige vaner.
- Miljø i hverdagen.
- Cykelkultur.
- Trafiksikkerhed.
- Alternativ energi.

Why: Official written example mentions traffic safety. Life in Aalborg reports alternative energy, everyday environment, and cycling culture. Learn Danish Lab lists climate and cycling transport.

Practice stance:

- Individual habits vs political regulation.
- Public transport, cycling, city planning, and cost.

## Medium Probability

### 6. Integration, Immigration, and Danish Society

Why: PD3 exists in a citizenship/residency/work context, and Learn Danish Lab lists immigration/integration. This is highly relevant but may be politically sensitive, so expect broad framing rather than a narrow ideology prompt.

### 7. Equality, Family, and Gender Roles

Why: Learn Danish PDF uses equality linked to childcare; Learn Danish Lab lists gender equality in the labor market. Strong B2 topic because it supports pros/cons and society-level argument.

### 8. Older People, Pensions, and Welfare State

Why: LWiD lists retirees; AB Danish lists pensions and welfare. Likely as a Part 2 picture/discussion topic.

### 9. Housing and Personal Economy

Why: Learn Danish Lab lists renting and housing. LWiD lists youth and finance. Good candidate because it supports personal experience plus social consequences.

### 10. Crime, Punishment, and Democracy

Why: A learner reported "alternative punishment"; AB Danish lists democracy. Good B2 topic, but less recurrent across sources than work/health/digital.

## Low-to-Medium Wildcards

- Tourism and sustainability.
- Entrepreneurship.
- Disability and inclusion.
- Public vs private sector.
- Volunteer work.
- Leadership.

## Preparation Priority

If time is short, prepare five reusable "master speeches":

1. Work and stress.
2. Social media and children.
3. Health and welfare.
4. School/youth.
5. Climate/transport.

Then prepare bridge vocabulary for the medium-probability topics so you can adapt under pressure.
